# Rebirth: Eclipse System — Story Overview

## Premise

Arden Vale was a surveyor-scout for the Ashgrave Expedition, a mixed-nation venture
sent to chart the Hollow Reach — a dead zone on every map, abandoned three centuries
ago after it swallowed an entire war. Six months into the expedition, Arden's unit
finds something that was never supposed to survive down there: a device the
expedition's own charter calls, in its driest bureaucratic language, "a Class-Zero
anomaly, origin indeterminate." Commander Thessaly Kade decides on the spot that the
anomaly is worth more buried than reported. Arden disagrees loud enough to be
remembered for it. Three days later, in the collapsed lower gallery of the ruin his
own unit was mapping, Arden is the one left behind when the charges go off.

He does not die cleanly. He dies with a question — *why would hiding it matter more
than what it does* — and the anomaly answers instead of the dark. The Eclipse System
activates, calls what's left of him "a soul anomaly of unknown compatibility," and
offers him a second body in a world that has never heard of the Hollow Reach, the
Ashgrave Expedition, or Thessaly Kade. It does not promise him power. It promises him
a system that is still, after everything, missing an owner it will admit to.

This is the engine of the whole story: Arden is not reborn as a chosen one. He is
reborn as an edge case a system is still trying to classify, in a body everyone
around him has already written off. Power comes slowly, and always at a cost the
story shows on-screen — a resistance capped, a memory absorbed that he didn't ask
for, a sync rate that rises only when he survives something it shouldn't have let
him survive.

## Tone

Dark fantasy with anime-serial pacing: short, loaded dialogue scenes; decisive
reveals telegraphed one or two arcs ahead of their payoff; combat that threatens real
loss (companions can die from player choices in Arcs V, VII and IX; none die "for
shock value" — every death is a consequence the player can trace back to a decision
or a missed warning). Romance is adult, optional, and never the main plot's
replacement — it changes dialogue, companion loyalty, and in two cases a late-game
ending branch, but the player who ignores it entirely still gets a complete story.

## The Three Questions the Whole Story Answers

1. **Who activated the Eclipse System before Arden, and what happened to them?**
   (Answered across Arcs VI, VIII, X.)
2. **Why did Thessaly Kade really order the anomaly buried?**
   (Answered across Arcs I, V, X — three different versions of the same hour, each
   less flattering to someone.)
3. **What is the System actually for?**
   (Answered in Arc VIII, recontextualized in Arc XII.)

## New-World Primer (for Arc II onward)

Arden wakes in **Veyth Hollow**, a border town of the **Thresh Dominion**, inside the
body of **Joren Calt**, an eighteen-year-old laborer's son everyone in Veyth Hollow
calls "the empty-mana boy" — tested at birth for mana aptitude and found, by every
instrument the Dominion owns, to have none. Joren's family is alive, poor, and real:
his decisions about them (support them, leave them, tell them the truth) are some of
the first weight the player carries. The world runs on a public, legible magic
system — **Aptitude Ranks F through S**, tested and licensed by the Dominion — which
is why "Rank F, Class: Unknown" reading on Arden's very first System panel is not
flavor text; it's the reason every authority figure in Arc II underestimates him on
sight, and the reason the story can earn every power gain that follows instead of
asserting it.
